Nine Stages of UAE Car Ownership
A channel about cars runs out of cars. A channel about owning a car in the UAE never does — every driver here walks the same nine stages, the rules barely move, and the country adds new residents who have not walked any of them yet. Five stages already have data behind them. Four are wide open.

Alongside it, the two checks every used buyer needs and few make — whether a bank still owns the car, and whether it has been written off before.

04
Register it
covered
AED 350 plus inspection at 170–200, so the real annual bill lands near 550–600. Miss the deadline and it is AED 500 a month — more than the registration itself.

“Forgetting for three months costs more than a year of registration.”
ownership.csv · already sourced

05 Insure it
your strongest ground
63 sourced rows, and almost none of it is common knowledge: excess capped by car value from AED 350 to 1,400, written off once repairs pass 50% of value, parts depreciated to 30% by year six, a 10% loading if the driver is under 25.

“Your insurer is not allowed to charge you more than this.”
insurance.csv + rules.csv · already sourced

06 Run it
covered
Salik at AED 6 peak, 4 off-peak, free between 1am and 6am — and no daily cap, which is the part that surprises people. Fuel is in there too, and by design those rows expire every month rather than quietly going wrong.

“There is no daily Salik cap. Here's what your commute actually costs.”
ownership.csv · already sourced

07 Survive the summer
nothing yet — best opening
Fifty degrees does things to a car that no global review channel has any reason to explain. The RTA treats tyres over five years old as unfit whatever the tread looks like; batteries die here in a fraction of the time they last elsewhere; the tint limit is a legal number, not a preference.

The climate does not change. Everything in this stage stays true for as long as the channel exists.

“Your tyres are illegal at five years old. The tread has nothing to do with it.”
needs research → heat.csv
08
Crash it, fix it
one row in, the rest open
Nobody searches this until the morning it happens, and then they search it urgently: the police report and why no garage will touch the car without one, agency repair against a back-street workshop, what the excess actually costs you.

One piece is already sourced and is the strongest single fact in the whole file — the at-fault driver's insurer owes you AED 300 a day, up to fifteen days, while your car sits in the workshop. Almost nobody claims it.

“Your insurer owes you 300 dirhams a day. Ask for it.”
rules.csv has this one · the rest needs research
09
Sell it, or leave
nothing yet — highest intent
The most emotionally loaded search in the whole lane, and one that exists almost nowhere else in the world: you are leaving the country and the bank still owns your car. Clearance certificates, what an unpaid loan can do to your ability to travel, selling a car with finance still on it, export plates, transferring ownership.

Every expat leaves eventually. This is a permanent audience that renews itself.

“Leaving the UAE with a car loan.”
needs research → exit.csv
